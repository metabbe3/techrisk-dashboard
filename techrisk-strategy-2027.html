Executive summary

Every pain point traces to one of four roots.

Our read of the FY2026 review: none of the seven pain points stands alone. They cluster into four root conditions, and the 2027 plan addresses the roots rather than the symptoms.

A

Capacity is guessed, coverage is partial

Planning never happened, and the fundamentals that prevent incidents — reviews, tests, a ranked backlog — grew by habit instead of design.

B

Responders show up, nobody owns the call

War rooms convene engineers but no designated leader, and after hours there is no standing team to catch what monitoring misses.

C

Incidents close, lessons don't

Investigation forms come in thin and late, corrective actions go untracked, and small fundlosses repeat until they grow. BI-FAS is the standing example.

D

Three processes run on memory

Fund-risk submissions, external RCA reporting and company-loss deadlines have no working instruction, no RACI and no date anyone is measured on.

Theme A · Capacity & coverage

Capacity is guessed, coverage is partial.

Nobody owns the transaction forecast, and the engineering fundamentals that prevent incidents are applied unevenly across the team.

The pain

PP-1Capacity planning was never conducted properly — no forecast of transaction targets behind staffing or infrastructure decisions
PP-2aMonitoring and escalation are thin for fundloss, non-fundloss and MOTO-CC incidents
PP-2bCode review and solution-architecture review are inconsistent
PP-2cTest coverage is low
PP-2dThe feature backlog has no priority management

The fix

I-01 · closes PP-1

Capacity forecast program

A standing quarterly cycle with the business team to forecast transaction targets, and size staffing and infrastructure against it.

I-02 · closes PP-2a

24/7 coverage model

A standing rota across TS, Security and IDC (system, network, DBA) plus SRE, with a defined escalation path at any hour.

I-03 · closes PP-2b–d

Engineering quality baseline

Code review and SA gates in the delivery flow, test coverage targets, and a ranked backlog with explicit priority rules.

Theme B · Incident command

The war room has responders. It needs a commander.

During incidents, engineers work their lanes but nobody owns the call. Decisions stall, priorities blur, and the room waits for someone to lead.

The pain

PP-3No designated leadership during incidents in the war room — no criteria exist for who should lead, so leadership is improvised or absent

The fix

I-04 · closes PP-3

Incident commander framework

Explicit criteria for who can lead an incident, empowerment to make the call, and training so every war room opens with a named IC.

Detect & triage → War room opens → Named IC owns the call → TS · Security · IDC · SRE execute

Theme D · Governance & process

Three processes run on memory.

Fund-risk submissions, external RCA reporting and company-loss deadlines have no documented owner, sequence or due date. Each gets a working instruction and a measure.
